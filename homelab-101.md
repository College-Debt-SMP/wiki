# Homelab 101

College Debt would not be possible without its server system which by itself is a homelab. A homelab is a self-ran server computer that runs fully headless (without a monitor, keyboard, or mouse) and is primarily for processing and serving internet content to client devices (such as your personal phone, laptop, or desktop). Major websites on the internet operates in roughly the same way.

### Advantage and disadvantage

A homelab enables self-hosting, which allows you to run applications fully under your control. This is beneficial in terms of:

* Privacy. Everything operates locally, and no companies can automatically have a peak at your data.
* Choice. There are community developed applications that serves as a free alternative to paid options, which can help you save on subscription costs.
* Possibility. You can use your server as a NAS, an ad blocking server, a smart home hub, or even for streaming games between devices at home. There are many things you can do with a homelab.

There are some initial hurdles though when getting started with a homelab.

* You will need to get a server of your own, which can either be free (if you already got the hardware or uses any of the free options) or very pricey depending on what you ended up buying.

#### Free option

If you don't have the resources to deploy your own homelab at home, that's ok. As a free alternative, you can sign up for an Oracle Cloud account and claim their [free Arm Compute Instance](https://www.oracle.com/cloud/free/) allowances (as of July 2026). With up to 2 cores, 12GB of RAM and 200GB of storage, it should be enough for a simple homelab server.

If you cannot create the free instance after getting your account set up, you will need to enable payment on your account with a card on file to bypass the restriction. As long as you don't use any other resources that falls outside of the free tier, you won't ever get charged.
